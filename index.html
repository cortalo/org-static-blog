For LTI system

\begin{aligned} y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t-\tau) x(\tau) d\tau \end{aligned}

if \(x(\tau) =\exp(j\omega \tau)\), let \(z = t-\tau, \tau = t-z\)

\begin{aligned} y(t) &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t-\tau) \exp(j\omega \tau) (-1)(-1) d\tau\\ &= \int_{\infty}^{-\infty} h(z)\exp(j\omega t)\exp(-j\omega z)(-1)dz\\ &= \exp(j\omega t) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(z)\exp(-j\omega z) dz\\ &= \exp(j\omega t) H(\omega) \end{aligned}

For LPTV system

\begin{aligned} y(t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} h(t,\tau) x(\tau) d\tau \end{aligned}

where \(h(t+T,\tau+T) = h(t,\tau)\), let \(z=t-\tau, g(t,z) = h(t,t-z)\), thus \(g(t,z)\) is period in \(t\), e.g., \(g(t+T,z) = g(t,z)\).

\begin{aligned} y(t) &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(t,z)\exp(j\omega t)\exp(-j\omega z) dz\\ &= \exp(j\omega t) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(t,z)\exp(-j\omega z) dz\\ &= \exp(j\omega t) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} g_n(z)\exp(-jn\omega_0 t) \exp(-j\omega z) dz\\ &= \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \exp(j\omega t)\exp(-jn\omega_0 t) \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g_n(z) \exp(-j\omega z) dz\\ &= \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \exp(j\omega t) \exp(-jn\omega_0 t) G_n(\omega) \end{aligned}

pnoise

For a simple testbench that a voltage noise source \(S_v(f)\) (one-sided spectrum) is added on top of a sin wave, when using the timeaverage pnoise with pm mode, assuming beat frequency \(f_0\), relative harmonic is 1, then the SSB output noise spectrum is \(S(f) = (S_v(f_0 + f) + S_v(f_0 - f))/2/2\), where the first divided by 2 is to average the \(S_v(f_0+f)\) and \(S_v(f_0-f)\), and the second divided by 2 is because for such a simple testbench, half of the power is for pm and another half is for am. And the DSM noise output will be simply twice of the SSB. The phase noise is the ratio between SSB noise output and carrier power.

However, if using sampled(jitter) mode, depends on pnoise maximum sidebands, aliasing will affect the simulation result. To properly address aliasing, let’s consider the two-sided spectrum of the voltage noise source \(S_v(f)/2\). Assuming the maximum sidebands is \(N\), then the band corresponding to 0 sideband is \([-f_0/2, f_0/2]\), corresponding to 1 sideband is \([f_0 - f_0/2, f_0 + f_0/2]\) and \([-f_0 - f_0/2, -f_0 + f_0/2]\),…,corresponding to \(N\) sideband is \([N*f_0 - f_0/2, N*f_0 + f_0/2]\) and \([-N*f_0 - f_0/2, -N*f_0 + f_0/2]\), add all of them together gives you the two-sided voltage noise spectrum, where the divided by 2 is because we are considering the \(S_v(f)/2\).

\begin{aligned} \sum_{i=-N}^{N} \dfrac{S_v(i*f_0 + f)}{2} \end{aligned}

and one-sided voltage noise spectrum

\begin{aligned} S(f) = \sum_{i=-N}^{N} \dfrac{S_v(i*f_0 + f)}{2} + \sum_{i=-N}^{N} \dfrac{S_v(i*f_0 - f)}{2} \end{aligned}

then the phase noise is

\begin{aligned} L(f) = \dfrac{1}{2} \dfrac{S_v(f)}{\alpha^2} \left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2 \end{aligned}

where the first divide by two is because converting the spectrum of phase to phase noise spectrum.
